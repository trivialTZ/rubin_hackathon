How to read this — and what it does not show
  • The two flux scales were reconciled, not read off. LSST psfFlux is nanojansky (AB ZP 31.4). The DEBaSS V4 files carry no ZP_FLUXCAL header, so SNANA's default ZP 27.5 was assumed and checked against LSST on bright epochs; conversion is fluxnJy = FLUXCAL × 36.31. If DEBaSS recalibrates, this factor must be re-derived.
  • The LSST image is a static deep coadd, not the epoch you selected. DP2 ships no per-epoch visit or difference images — those arrive with full DP2 (Oct–Dec 2026). The image does not change as you move along the timeline.
  • DECam pixels are absent, not missing. The exposures exist at NOIRLab but are proprietary until 2026-12-29.
  • Pairing is same-band and nearest-neighbour within ± d. One LSST epoch can be claimed by more than one DECam epoch, so pairs are not a strict matching. Where that happens, the partner shown is the temporally nearest.
  • DECam epochs failing SNANA's photometry flags are excluded. Rows with PHOTFLAG & 1016 (flux-fit failure, off-CCD, masked, PSF failure, no stamp) are dropped — 62 of 336 across the sample. They are failed measurements, not faint ones.
  • Sign convention: Δm = mLSST − mDECam, so positive means LSST measured the source fainter.
  • Reliability dominates the comparison. Over the 59 same-band pairs, the median LSST/DECam flux ratio is 0.164 — but that is almost entirely low-quality detections. Split by DP2 reliability: ratio 0.034 (Δm +2.58 mag) below 0.1, versus ratio 0.704 (Δm +0.38 ± 0.33 mag) above 0.9. All 19 high-reliability pairs have positive flux in both surveys, so that figure is not distorted by the positive-flux cut. Treat low-reliability points as spurious rather than as disagreement between the surveys.
  • The residual +0.38 mag at high reliability is unexplained. It may be aperture/PSF differences or DECam template contamination. Do not treat it as a validated cross-calibration.
